# Decoder material provenance and preceding generated-word XOR

K and the incoming payload are distinct arguments at the proven boundaries. One selector supplies an image-backed 56-byte span; three selectors obtain material through retained-state virtual getters. The decoder hashes K to seed a deterministic 312-word generator, XORs incoming bytes with its generated words, and feeds that result to the subsequent derived-key/block-XOR stream. This report does not recover assets or implement a standalone decoder.

## Byte flow and chunk continuity

XOR-stream virtual read0x4dd600 first asks its borrowed raw stream to fill the destination span, propagates raw EOF to its own flag, advances by the actual returned count, then calls0x4df880 with source=destination for in-place XOR. The generator pointer passed is A+8. Payload bytes cannot reseed the generator on this path.

0x4df880 returns on zero length and checks requested count against the provided capacity. Whole8-byte groups call0x43bfe0 and XOR the returned qword with input at0x4df9b0..0x4df9bf. On this little-endian target this consumes the low byte first. A remaining1..7 bytes obtains one additional word, stores it atG+0x9c8 and records the consumed count atG+0x9d0. A later read first uses the remaining cached bytes and clears the count after all8 are used. Thus the generated byte sequence continues across arbitrary read chunk boundaries; it does not restart per refill.

These output bytes are the input of the buffered block-XOR layer documented in decoder-stream-contract. Both layers derive their state from K; the original payload bytes remain the data being transformed. No authentication, secrecy or successful processing follows solely from this dataflow.
